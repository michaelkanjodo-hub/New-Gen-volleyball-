Start the rally on your terms.

A repeatable toss and a clean contact matter more than a hard swing. Build accuracy before power.

01

The controlled float serve

Stand balanced behind the baseline. Toss just in front of your hitting shoulder and meet the middle of the ball with a firm, flat hand. A clean, spin-free contact creates the float.

  1. Serve to a target in the back metre of the opposite court.
  2. Keep the toss at a consistent height, slightly in front.
  3. Finish with your weight moving toward the target.
Practice target · 3 sets of 10 serves, recording hits on target
02

Build the jump-serve sequence

Learn the toss, approach, and contact separately before combining them. Contact high and in front, with your wrist moving over the ball to create topspin.

  1. Practise a low, controlled toss without a serve.
  2. Add your approach and catch the ball at contact height.
  3. Serve at reduced power, then increase effort as the toss becomes consistent.
Coach check · Ask for feedback on your toss and landing

Find your approach.

Good attacking starts with your feet. Learn the rhythm slowly, then add height and speed.

01

The three-step approach

For a right-handed attacker, the usual three-step rhythm is left–right–left. The final two steps arrive quickly to help transfer forward movement into a controlled jump. Reverse the footwork if you are left-handed.

  1. Start with a directional left step.
  2. Take a longer right step and bring your arms back.
  3. Close with the left foot, swing your arms up, and jump.
Practice target · 10 slow approaches without a ball
02

A clean arm swing

Keep your non-hitting hand on the ball’s line. Rotate through your trunk, reach high, and contact in front of your hitting shoulder. Land softly on both feet with your knees aligned.

  • Start with controlled standing hits against a wall.
  • Progress to a coach-fed ball over the net.
  • Aim for placement before adding power.
Coach check · Stop if shoulder or knee pain develops

Make the first contact count.

Move your feet early and let a steady platform do the work.

01

A reliable forearm platform

Bring your hands together with thumbs parallel and elbows extended. Keep the forearms level, bend your knees, and angle the platform toward your setter.

  1. Shuffle into line before the ball arrives.
  2. Contact on the forearms, not the wrists.
  3. Pass to a stationary target without swinging your arms.
Practice target · 3 rounds of 10 controlled partner passes
02

Dig with control

Start low and balanced. Read the attacker, move behind the ball, and aim your first contact high toward the middle of the court rather than trying to win the point immediately.

  • Have a partner send balls to your left and right.
  • Reset to a balanced position after every dig.
  • Keep the ball off the net so your setter can use it.
Practice target · 5 controlled digs to the setter’s area

Give your attackers a chance.

Footwork, balance, and soft hands turn a pass into a hittable set.

01

Soft hands, clean release

Shape your hands above your forehead with fingers spread. Receive and release with the finger pads, using your legs to support a smooth, brief contact. Avoid slapping or holding the ball.

  1. Start with small sets straight above your head.
  2. Keep your elbows comfortably apart and wrists relaxed.
  3. Progress to setting a self-tossed ball to a target.
Practice target · 20 balanced overhead sets
02

Get underneath the ball

Move quickly from the net, stop with your feet balanced, and face your target before contact. A consistent body position makes your sets easier to read and hit.

  • Use a partner’s pass rather than a perfect toss.
  • Set to the outside, then reset your feet.
  • Ask your attacker whether the ball is consistently playable.
Coach check · Let your coach assess any prolonged contact

Be ready at the net.

Read the play, move efficiently, and time your jump to the attacker.

01

Move, balance, then jump

Stay ready with your hands up. Shuffle for short distances and use a crossover step for longer moves. Brake before jumping so you travel upward rather than into the net.

  1. Practise two shuffle steps and a balanced stop.
  2. Add a vertical jump with hands reaching above the net.
  3. Land under control and return to your ready position.
Practice target · 3 sets of 5 controlled block jumps
02

Read the setter and attacker

Watch the first pass, the setter’s body position, and the attacker’s approach. React to the actual set rather than guessing early. Keep your hands firm and reach into the attacking space when appropriate.

  • Start with a predictable set and focus on timing.
  • Have your coach vary the target once the rhythm is comfortable.
  • Keep a safe distance from the net when landing.
Coach check · Practise net safety before competitive blocking

Build a game that lasts.

Train movement quality before volume. Rest, technique, and a sensible progression are part of the work.

01

Jump and land well

Begin with low pogo jumps and controlled countermovement jumps. Focus on soft landings and knees tracking over toes. Add volume only when the movement stays controlled.

Starting point · 2 sets of 5 jumps with full recovery
02

Court agility

Use short shuffles between three court markers. Change direction under control, keeping your chest up and feet active. Walk back between repetitions.

Starting point · 5 short repetitions, resting as needed
03

Core and balance

Use a comfortable plank variation and a controlled single-leg balance. Stop each set before your position breaks down. A coach can help adapt these movements to your needs.

Starting point · 3 controlled holds on each side
04

Match-friendly conditioning

Alternate short, purposeful movement with recovery. Use court shuffles or easy running rather than an all-out sprint on your first session. Increase the workload gradually.

Starting point · 6 short efforts with comfortable recovery

Warm up first. These are general practice ideas, not individual medical advice. Work with a qualified coach, adjust for your experience, and stop if you feel pain.
